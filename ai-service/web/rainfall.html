⚙️ How it works

The question: how much water must the farmer add each month, so that the crop gets what it needs?
  1. The rain (AI forecast). We downloaded the real rain of every day since 2000 for the 24 governorates (Open-Meteo / ERA5). Months already passed show the real rain (up to October 2026). For the next months, an AI time-series model, SARIMA, learns the yearly rhythm of each region (wet winter, dry summer) from 26 years of monthly rain and forecasts the rain up to December 2027 (dashed line on the chart). One model per governorate.
    Tested on the past (3 periods × 24 governorates: real data hidden, 14 months forecast, then compared): error on the 14-month total 11 % (simple monthly average: 14 %); month by month ±17 mm.
  2. How much water the weather takes (ET0). Sun, heat, wind and dry air pull water out of the soil and the leaves. ET0 is this amount for a reference grass field, in mm per month. Open-Meteo computes it with the official FAO-56 formula from real weather (past months); for future months we use the usual value of that month (2010–2025).
  3. How thirsty the crop is (Kc). Each crop drinks more or less than grass, and it changes with age: small after planting, highest when adult, lower before harvest. These values come from the FAO-56 tables (e.g. tomato 0.60 → 1.15 → 0.80). After harvest Kc = 0.
  4. The crop's need: ETc = ET0 × Kc (mm per month).
  5. Useful rain: about 80 % of the rain reaches the roots; the rest runs off or evaporates: useful rain = 0.8 × rain.
  6. Water to add: water = ETc − useful rain (never below 0). In m³ per hectare: × 10 (1 mm of water on 1 hectare = 10 m³).
Example (the table above): if the crop needs 128 mm in April and 51.9 mm of rain falls, useful rain = 41.5 mm, so the farmer adds 128 − 41.5 = 86.8 mm = 868 m³ per hectare that month (≈ 29 m³/ha per day).
