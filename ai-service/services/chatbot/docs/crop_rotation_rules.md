# Crop rotation rules - rotation des cultures, succession, assolement (sourced)

Sources (read 21 November 2026):
- Clemson University HGIC Factsheet 1330 "Crop Rotation" (2021): https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/crop-rotation/
- University of California IPM, Tomato Pest Management Guidelines (UC ANR Publication 3470), crop rotation: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/tomato/crop-rotation/
- University of California IPM, Potato Pest Management Guidelines (UC ANR Publication 3463), crop rotation: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/potato/crop-rotation/
- SARE "Crop Rotation on Organic Farms: A Planning Manual" (Mohler & Johnson, 2009, reprinted 2020): file rotation_sare_organic_farms_manual.pdf

## The main rule - la règle principale: rotate plant FAMILIES, wait 3 years

- Rotate plant families, not only crops: crops of the same family share the same diseases and pests (Clemson).
- A crop (and its family) should be grown in the same spot at most once every 3 years; a 4- or 5-year rotation is even better (Clemson).
- Rotation does not help against diseases carried by wind or insects that survive on weeds; use separate plots and clean tools between plots (Clemson).

## Plant families of Tunisian crops - familles botaniques (Clemson table)

- Solanaceae (nightshade family / solanacées): tomato (tomate), pepper (piment, poivron), eggplant (aubergine), potato (pomme de terre). Never follow one by another.
- Cucurbitaceae (cucurbits / cucurbitacées): watermelon (pastèque), melon / cantaloupe, cucumber (concombre), squash, zucchini (courgette), pumpkin.
- Amaryllidaceae (onion family / alliacées): onion (oignon), garlic (ail), leek (poireau).
- Apiaceae (parsley family / apiacées): carrot (carotte), parsley, celery, coriander, fennel (fenouil).
- Asteraceae (sunflower family / astéracées): lettuce (laitue), artichoke (artichaut), sunflower.
- Brassicaceae (cabbage family / crucifères): cabbage (chou), cauliflower, broccoli, turnip (navet), radish, canola (colza).
- Leguminosae / Fabaceae (bean family / légumineuses): bean (haricot), pea (petit pois), peanut, and also chickpea (pois chiche), broad bean / faba bean (fève), lentil (lentille), alfalfa (luzerne) are legumes.
- Poaceae (grass family / céréales, graminées): wheat (blé), barley (orge), oats, maize / corn (maïs), sorghum (sorgho), rice.
- Amaranthaceae (beet family): beet, sugar beet (betterave), spinach (épinard), Swiss chard.

## Tomato - tomate: what to plant before and after (UC IPM table)

Avoid planting tomatoes in the same field year after year; rotating to a nonhost crop reduces pests.
Suggested rotation by problem (pest | years away from tomato | nonhost crops):
- Verticillium wilt (verticilliose) | 3 years | small grains (wheat, barley), corn (maize)
- Phytophthora root rot | 1 year | cereals for severe infestations
- Bacterial spot | 1 year or more | non-solanaceous crops
- Bacterial canker | 1 year or more | non-solanaceous crops
- Fusarium wilt (fusariose) | 2 years or more | crops other than tomato
- Southern blight | 3 years | small grains
- Corky root rot | 2 years or more | small grains, corn
- Root-knot nematode | - | resistant tomato varieties and non-host crops
- Winter weeds: wheat or small grains in fall; summer weeds: corn in rotation with tomato.
Best crops after tomato: cereals (wheat, barley, maize, sorghum) and legumes (beans, peas, chickpea, faba bean). Do not follow tomato with pepper, eggplant or potato (same family).

## Potato - pomme de terre (UC IPM table)

Avoid planting potatoes in the same field year after year; avoid solanaceous crops in the rotation; control volunteer potatoes and weeds.
- Bacterial ring rot | 1+ year | non-potato crop
- Black dot | 3+ years | non-solanaceous crop
- Common scab (gale commune) | 1+ year | cereals; green manure cover crops (rye, millet, oats)
- Late blight (mildiou) | 1+ year | non-solanaceous crop
- Powdery scab | 3+ years | non-solanaceous crop
- Silver scurf | 1+ year | non-solanaceous crop
- Stem and stolon canker (rhizoctone) | 2-3 years | non-potato crop but not sugar beet
- Stem rot | 3 years | small grains
- Verticillium wilt | 2-3 years | cereals, grasses, legumes
- White mold | 1+ year | cereals, grasses
- Wireworms | 3+ years | alfalfa
- Northern root-knot nematode | 2+ years | small grains

## What to plant after a crop - quelle culture planter après (the family rule applied to Tunisian crops)

Rule (Clemson): the next crop must be from a DIFFERENT family, and the same family comes back only after 3 years.
Legumes are best placed BEFORE a demanding crop because they leave nitrogen (SARE manual).
- After watermelon, melon, cucumber, zucchini (cucurbits / cucurbitacées): wheat or barley (cereal), chickpea or faba
  bean (legume), onion or garlic, carrot, tomato or pepper. NOT another cucurbit for 3 years (not melon after watermelon).
- After tomato, pepper, eggplant, potato (Solanaceae): cereals (wheat, barley, maize, sorghum) or legumes (UC IPM).
  NOT another Solanaceae (no potato after tomato).
- After chickpea, faba bean, lentil, pea, bean, alfalfa (legumes): a demanding crop that uses their nitrogen:
  wheat, barley, tomato, potato, melon / watermelon.
- After wheat, barley, oats, maize, sorghum (cereals): legumes (chickpea, faba bean) to give back nitrogen, or
  vegetables (tomato, potato, melon, onion).
- After onion, garlic, leek (onion family): legumes, cereals, Solanaceae or cucurbits; NOT onion or garlic again.
- After carrot (Apiaceae), lettuce (Asteraceae), cabbage (Brassicaceae): any crop of another family, a legume is a good choice.

## Example rotations - exemples de rotation

- 3-year rotation (Clemson): beans (legume) -> tomatoes (Solanaceae) -> corn (Poaceae) -> beans again. Three different families.
- 4-year rotation (Clemson), each bed changes every year: tomatoes -> corn -> okra -> beans.
- Tunisian crops applying the same rule (3 different families, legume before the demanding crop):
  chickpea or faba bean (legume) -> tomato or potato (Solanaceae) -> wheat or barley (cereal) -> then melon / watermelon (cucurbit) or onion (Amaryllidaceae) the next year.
  Legumes before a demanding crop are a classic choice because legumes fix nitrogen (see the SARE manual).

## What these sources do NOT give

- Exact Tunisian rotation recommendations by governorate: ask the CRDA (Commissariat Régional au Développement Agricole) of your governorate.
- Rotation rules for fruit trees (olive, citrus, dates): these are perennial crops, not rotated.
